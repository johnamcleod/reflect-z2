# Open questions and unverified steps

Each item lists what we would need to see before we stop treating it as an assumption.

## OQ1: the n → n+1 step (non-reflective at n ⇒ non-reflective at n+1). RESOLVED by our own proof

- **Primary citation.** Bugaenko 1990, Thm 2 (thesis Thm 3.0.8). That paper has not been read, because the PDF is unavailable.
- **Self-contained proof over ℤ[√2].** This is Mark's Prop. 3.1 argument, re-checked for O_K = ℤ[√2]. Let L⁺ = L^{(n+1)}, P⁺ its chamber, and e = −v_{n+1}. Then e is a root of norm 1 and a facet of P⁺, since it is a stabiliser simple root.
  1. **Angles at e.** Let r be a facet root of P⁺ with norm m, adjacent to e, and put t = (e, r) ∈ O_K, which holds because the form is integral on L⁺. The crystallographic condition 2t/m ∈ O_K lets us write t = m·u/2 with u ∈ O_K, so cos² ∠(e, r) = t²/m = m·u²/4.
     - π/3 needs m·u² = 1, so m = u⁻², and then t = 1/(2u) ∉ O_K. Impossible.
     - π/6 needs m·u² = 3, and then t = 3/(2u) ∉ O_K. Impossible.
     - π/8 needs m·u² = 2+√2, and then t = (2+√2)/(2u) = ε/(√2·u) ∉ O_K. Impossible.
     - So the angle is π/2 or π/4.
  2. **The walls of F = P⁺ ∩ e^⊥ come from roots of L = e^⊥ ∩ L⁺.**
     - If the angle is π/2, then r ⊥ e and r ∈ L, and r is a root of L (the crystallographic condition restricts).
     - If the angle is π/4, then m·u² = 2 and t = 1/u. The vector r′ = r − t·e lies in L, is orthogonal to e, and has norm m − t² = u⁻². It is primitive, of unit-square norm, and the crystallographic condition holds trivially. Its mirror meets e^⊥ exactly where r's mirror does.
  3. **No root of L cuts F.** Every root x of L is a root of L⁺, because (x, y + c·v_{n+1}) = (x, y). So its mirror misses int P⁺. The mirror x^⊥ is perpendicular to e^⊥, so it would cross int P⁺ near any relative-interior point of F that it contained. Hence F is the chamber of W(L) containing v₀.
  4. **Conclusion.** If L⁺ is reflective, P⁺ has finite volume, so F has finite volume, so W(L) has finite covolume and L is reflective. By contraposition, non-reflective at n gives non-reflective at n+1.
- **Status.** Resolved. The proof uses only the integrality of the form, the crystallographic condition, and the absence of k = 3, 6, 8 angles at a norm-1 root over ℤ[√2]. All three facts are checked above, and the angle computation is also tested in `tests/test_extension.py`. Bugaenko's theorem is cited as corroboration only.

## OQ2: elliptic subdiagrams ↔ faces for the infinitely-faceted chamber

- **Use.** The certificate argument (methods.md, Phase 3, step 4) needs this: if a set S of n facets of the W-chamber P has an elliptic Gram matrix, then the mirrors of S meet at a vertex of P, and the cone of P at that vertex is cut out by S alone.
- **Status.** Vinberg proved this for acute-angled polyhedra; the thesis states it as Prop. 2.2.9 for Coxeter polytopes. For the chamber of a discrete reflection group with infinitely many facets, local finiteness of the mirror arrangement gives a neighbourhood of the point v_S where only finitely many mirrors pass, and Vinberg's argument applies to that finite local arrangement.
  - We think this is fine, but we have not found a reference that states it for infinitely many facets.
  - For S₀ (the v₀ vertex) the claim is immediate: the stabiliser of v₀ is generated by the B_n reflections, so the cone of P at v₀ is the B_n chamber.
  - For the target vertex S′ the claim is the one that matters.
  - **General base vertex** (used for p47b, n = 2): the base S is then also a found elliptic set rather than S₀. So the claim is needed at both ends. It is the same claim, and the verifier re-establishes both S and S′ as facets of P.

## OQ3: reflective super-lattices (requirement 7b)

- **Result.** No reflective super-lattice L′ ⊃ L was found for any certified case.
- **Why that is not a negative result.** The search is bounded:
  - pure translations among words of length ≤ 2;
  - at most 3 distinct L′ per case;
  - small Vinberg caps on each L′.
- **Effectively untested.** Some large-index L′ hit their time limit before any root was found, and p31b and p41a (n = 3) exceeded the 1500 s case budget.
- **To close the question.** A conclusive answer needs either a proof that no L′ arising from such a decomposition is reflective, or much larger runs.

## OQ4: 522 table entries vs "432 lattices" (Mark, arXiv:1512.01133)

- **Observation.** The abstract and Theorem 1 say 432 lattices. The appendix of the TeX source (v1, the only arXiv version) has 522 entries, none commented out, and all 522 pass our exact consistency checks (tests/test_mark_table.py).
- **Duplicates exist.** The table lists some lattices several times. For example, ⟨−(−1+2√2),1,1⟩ is isometric to 6 different entries; see data/isometries/.
- **Unresolved.** Whether the duplicates account for exactly 90 = 522 − 432 is not checked. Checking needs full root cycles for the "(…)²" entries, whose tables list only half the roots.
- **Effect on the classification.** None. The classification uses only that every reflective SSF lattice appears *at least once*. The published version (Math. Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc. 164 (2018)) was not consulted. If it differs, it should be compared.

## OQ5: isometry calibration relies on Mark's chambers

- **What a missing match would prove.** If no entry is isometric to ⟨−d,1,1⟩, that is a proof of non-isometry only if each entry's listed roots are simple roots of a chamber of that lattice.
- **What we check instead.** We only check consistency: the listed vectors are roots, pairwise non-obtuse, with the printed angles.
- **Why this does not affect the classification.** The classification decides every candidate d directly with our own Vinberg runs and certificates. Positive matches are explicit isometries, checked exactly.

## OQ6: Bogachev's rank-4 table and an AlVin issue with non-reduced coefficients

**Source.** Bogachev, "From geometry to arithmetic of compact hyperbolic Coxeter polytopes", arXiv:2003.11944, Table "tab:stably-details". It lists the sub-2-reflective (stably reflective) Lorentzian lattices of signature (3,1) over ℤ[√2]. Here "stable" means (e,e) | 2, i.e. root norms 1, 2 and 2+√2.

**How the diagonal rows match our grid at n = 3:**

| Bogachev | d | our case | agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| L(1) | 1+√2 | unit | identical root sets |
| L(2) | 1+2√2 | p7b | identical root sets |
| L(3) | 5+4√2 | p7a | identical root sets |
| L(5) | √2 | ram2 | identical root sets |
| L(4) | 11+8√2 = (1+2√2)ε² | isometric to L(2), via x₀ ↦ εx₀ | see below |
| L(9) | 7+5√2 = (1+√2)ε² | isometric to L(1) | see below |

**The two problem rows.**
- The root lists for L(4) and L(9) differ from the L(2) and L(1) chambers.
- AlVin at our pinned commit reproduces them: ⟨−(7+5√2),1,1,1⟩ gives Bogachev's L(9) list, and ⟨−(11+8√2),1,1,1⟩ gives 17 roots, whose first 9 are Bogachev's L(4) list.
- In both cases AlVin misses the root r = (√2−1, 1+√2, 0, 0) of L_D. It is non-obtuse to the stabiliser roots, its δ (0.707, resp. 1.914) is smaller than that of AlVin's first accepted root, and it is obtuse to that root. So Vinberg's algorithm must accept r first.
- **Likely cause:** AlVin skips k₀ with 0 < k₀ < 1 at id. This matters only when the coefficient is not reduced modulo unit squares.
- **Our code** gives the same chamber for D and for the reduced d (tests/test_calibration.py::test_unit_square_rescaling_gives_same_chamber).

**Consequences.**
- Our AlVin cross-checks all use reduced d (`canonical`), so they are unaffected. Guglielmetti's 5+4√2 is non-reduced too, but AlVin agrees with us there.
- Bogachev's table then appears to contain 5 distinct lattices, not 7. Its sub-2-reflectivity conclusions for L(4) and L(9) still hold, because they are isometric to L(2) and L(1).
- This has not been reported to the authors. The Doklady paper itself was not accessible.
